### Introduction

---

SceneMaker is currently under development for eight months, from May to December 2026, as part of the “2026 Open-Source AI and Software Development and Utilization Support Project” sponsored by the Ministry of Science and ICT and the National IT Industry Promotion Agency. This post summarizes where we currently stand in the project timeline, what we will be doing during the remaining two months, and what we plan to continue after the project concludes.

The project timeline is divided into three phases. As of late October 2026, we have completed Phase 2 and are moving on to Phase 3.

![image](/img/notes/향후-로드맵-및-지속-기여-계획-공유-1건/img-00.jpg)

---

#### 1. So Far — Phases 1 and 2

**Phase 1 (May–August) — Core Engine and Service Prototype**

- We set up a GPU server and created an environment to serve OpenWAT models directly from our own server. We do not rely on commercial APIs.
- We sequentially developed speech analysis to convert broadcast audio into dialogue tagged with speaker and timestamps; visual analysis to describe the screen on a per-cut basis; and an indexing system to make these results searchable.
- We modeled the programming process—which converts a single question into a list of clip segments—using a LangGraph graph.

**Phase 2 (September–October) — Integration and Validation**

- We processed all 10 episodes of the SBS drama “Manager Kim” through the entire workflow, from registration to subtitling, screen analysis, and indexing.
- We built three service features on top of the same scheduling process: **Short Form**, which selects scenes from a single episode; **Collected Views**, which gathers scenes on a single topic from multiple episodes; and **Ad Spot Scheduling**, which identifies optimal points for inserting commercials.
- The scheduling process culminated in compositing, where segments were cut from the original footage and stitched together into a single MP4 file.
- The drama processing workflow, which had been running on experimental code, was migrated to the main repository at the end of October. The structure was organized by adding one module per genre, creating room to integrate other genres.

The operations for each stage are summarized in previous posts (audio analysis and processing, explanation of the short-form auto-generation pipeline, implementation of the “Compilation” service, and explanation of context-based ad optimization technology).

---

#### 2. The Remaining Two Months — Phase 3 (November–December)

**Certified Testing**

The project’s results are validated through external certified testing. There are two test criteria.

- **Processing Time:** Process a one-hour broadcast within 20 minutes.
- **Scene Analysis Accuracy:** The F1 score for scene classification must be 0.70 or higher for videos 40–60 minutes long, and 0.65 or higher for videos 60–120 minutes long.

Until now, measurements have been taken separately for each stage. In Stage 3, a single video is processed from start to finish in one go and measured as a whole.

**Official Open-Source Release**

- The service repositories are released under the Apache 2.0 license in the SceneMakerAI organization on GitHub.
- Prior to the release, we established operational guidelines for the main repositories. We documented who reviews and merges code, what permissions they have, how decisions are made, and where to report security issues.
- With each release, a list of components included in that version is automatically generated, and the licenses and known vulnerabilities are checked. If any items exceed the established criteria, the results are displayed in the release notes.
- Prompt templates used for broadcast analysis and datasets that can be publicly shared are uploaded to Hugging Face.

**Field Testing and Documentation**

- We incorporate feedback received from actual use in the broadcasting industry.
- We aim to publish a total of 20 posts on this technical blog.

---

#### 3. After the Project Ends — Plans for Continued Contribution

Although the project ends in December, the open-source code and contributions will continue afterward. We have four main plans.

![image](/img/notes/향후-로드맵-및-지속-기여-계획-공유-1건/img-01.jpg)

**Keep the Code Open and Improve It Together**

- Accept issues and pull requests submitted to the public repository. Review and release them according to the operational guidelines established earlier.
- The next review of the operational guidelines is scheduled for October 2027.

**Give Back to the Open-Source Projects We Use**

- SceneMaker runs on top of many open-source projects, such as model serving, speech processing, and vector search. We’ll reproduce any issues encountered while using these tools, make small fixes, and propose them to the original projects along with our tests.
- We will continue exactly as we have during the project period. This approach is outlined in the article “Comprehensive Guide to Open Source Contributions.”

**Sharing Broadcast AI Materials**

- When analyzing broadcast footage, I’ll disclose what and how I prompt the models, as well as which models I use and their configurations.
- Since broadcast footage and dialogue involve copyright and personal information, I’ll only upload data after verifying what can be publicly shared.

**Documenting and Engaging**

- I’ll continue writing a technical blog every other week. Posts will be written in Korean and published alongside English translations.
- I’ll share my experiences applying open models to the broadcasting domain in the Qwen and LangChain communities, as well as at domestic developer meetups.

---

### Conclusion

---

SceneMaker has now reached the stage where, given a single drama, it can generate short-form content, compilations, and even ad placements based on a single question. I will spend the remaining two months validating this through a certified exam and releasing the code and resources. After the project is complete, I plan to maintain a public repository, contribute back to the open-source projects I use, and continue sharing the resources and experience needed to build broadcasting AI.
